Speed-reading culture treats books as things to be *finished* rather than things to be *inhabited*. But the books that change you are rarely the ones you race through. They're the ones you argue with in the margins.

Here are three habits that made reading slower, and better, for me.

## 1. Underline by hand

Highlighting with a finger on a screen is friction-free, which is exactly the problem. The physical act of drawing a line under a sentence asks a tiny question: *is this worth the ink?* That pause is where attention lives.

## 2. Keep a margin index

At the end of each chapter, I write the two or three best lines in the back of the book, with page numbers. Weeks later I can find the argument again without rereading everything. It's a poor man's search — and the act of *choosing* the lines is the real payoff.

## 3. Reread what matters

A book I love gets read three times: once for the plot or the argument, once to notice the machinery, and once as a conversation. Each pass is a different book. You don't finish great books; you return to them.

> Reading is not the consumption of information. It is the slow construction of a self, one margin note at a time.

None of this requires special software or a system. Just a pencil and the willingness to be interrupted by your own thoughts.
